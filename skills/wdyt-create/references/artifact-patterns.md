# Artifact patterns

Choose the artifact by the decision the human needs to make, not by the format of the source material.

| Human job | Starting pattern | Make the first view answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand or edit a proposal, brief, PRD, or plan | Working document | What is this, why now, and what needs a decision? |
| Compare options or change assumptions | Decision/scenario model | Which inputs matter and how does the outcome move? |
| Tell a story in a room | Presentation | What is the narrative and where should discussion happen? |
| Evaluate a flow or product idea | Product walkthrough | What can I try, and what question should I answer? |
| Monitor performance | Dashboard | What changed, what is healthy, and what needs attention? |
| Collect structured input | Worksheet or workshop | What should each participant contribute or choose? |

## Working document

Use document-like width, strong typographic hierarchy, a visible title/status area, and short scannable sections. Make user-editable content explicit through normal controls or `data-wdyt-field`. Do not imitate a word processor toolbar unless the task actually needs rich formatting.

## Decision or scenario model

Separate assumptions, calculated outcomes, and interpretation. Show units beside inputs. Recalculate immediately. Include a plain-language takeaway, not only numbers. Use a data grid only when rows, sorting, filtering, or repeated editing justify it.

## Presentation

Design for one idea per scene. Keep slide navigation obvious but quiet. Make Present mode useful. Avoid shrinking a document into cards. Use large type, deliberate pacing, and visual variety across scenes.

## Product walkthrough

Implement the smallest believable interaction that answers the evaluation question. Preserve navigation state where it helps. Label simulated behavior honestly. Do not add fake authentication, payment, or destructive actions merely for realism.

## Dashboard

Prioritize status and change over decorative charts. Give every metric a label, unit, timeframe, and interpretation. If sample data is used, say so in the artifact.

## Workshop or worksheet

Make the next action unmistakable. Prefer a few durable fields over a complex form builder. Do not collect sensitive responses on public-by-link pages.

## Quality bar

- Open with meaningful content, never lorem ipsum or unexplained placeholders.
- Fit the user's brand or context rather than reusing one visual language.
- Use motion to clarify arrival, change, or focus; respect reduced motion.
- Make keyboard focus visible.
- Check narrow layouts; never rely on horizontal scrolling for primary content.
- Keep instructions near the action that needs them.
- Let WDYT comments handle review; do not recreate a second comment system inside the artifact.
